Against this, the grant allows €2.0M plus up to €1.0M additional
funding. Since the 2026 Work Programme, additional funding may cover
"any cost category" including personnel, but it must be shown to be
necessary for the proposed work, and both the base amount and the
additional request are assessed in evaluation.

**Options for closing the gap (PI decision, see TODO 1):**

- *A. Keep the full programme with ≈€1.0M additional funding.* Bekavac
  and Grau are funded in kind from running projects (saving about
  €475k including indirect costs), which brings the total to about
  €3.0M, right at the combined ceiling; trimming the student-assistant
  line absorbs the last €10k. The additional request would be
  justified by the legal island: Postdoc B and PhD 3 carry Island L and
  the cross-panel legal validation (about €800k including indirect
  costs), with the remainder covering part of the engineer.
- *B. Stay within €2.0M.* This allows direct costs of at most about
  €1.54M. With Bekavac and Grau in kind, it funds Postdoc A, PhD 1,
  PhD 2, and a reduced engineer, and drops Postdoc B and PhD 3, so
  Island L rests on Bekavac alone through M20, which weakens WP5 and
  the SH2 cross-panel case.

Personnel dominates because the project's cost is people-time on
protocol design, proofs, and two long-running field deployments. The
infrastructure line is modest by design: WP3's benchmarking needs real multi-party topologies and little compute, and both islands run
on partner infrastructure that already exists.
