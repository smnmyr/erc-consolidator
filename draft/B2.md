**Integration.** Each project year closes with an integration
milestone at which we build the WP3 and WP4 prototypes into a single
running *island* stack and deploy it at one or more WP5 sites. Each
milestone produces a versioned reference implementation, a revised
WP1 account and decision record, and a written *calculus
reconciliation*, which records for every architectural decision taken
that year whether it was derivable from the current WP2 model or
forced a revision of it. The reconciliation makes the theory
falsifiable once a year, on a schedule fixed in advance.

**Layering.** WP1 fixes the NFP bundles; WP2 names them as link
classes; WP3 defines how each class is upgraded, downgraded, or
refused at a border; WP4 supplies the addressing and *transclusion*
substrate (T4.1), turns SMPC-based resolution into an addressing
primitive that the link-class typing can name (T4.2), and exposes both
to agent clients as state-dependent *affordances*, or binds them at
design time as *Static Links* (T4.3). WP5 instantiates the whole in
islands whose threat models, stakes, and governance differ; this
difference is what allows the WP2 model to claim generality.

**Openness.** We release all software as open source under a
permissive licence, submit all specifications to the W3C groups that
maintain the protocols islands build on, or to their successors (Linked
Web Storage WG, Social Web WG and CG, Verifiable Credentials and
Federated Identity WGs, Web of Things WG, Autonomous Agents on the Web
CG; RFC 9518 advises reusing such proven mechanisms), publish open
access, and publish all benchmark harnesses and datasets that are not
legally restricted alongside the papers.

### 2.2 WP2 — Link classes and the guarantee calculus (M6–M60)

*Method: formal modeling with design-science iteration.*

**Construction.** WP2 isolates the *link classes* that the WP1 *islands*
require and orders them in a lattice of guarantee bundles. We annotate
each bundle with (i) its minimal coordination requirements, expressed
as standing obligations on island members (registration, replication
factor, identity binding, availability coupling); (ii) the threat
models under which it is admissible; and (iii) its cost vector.
Information-flow control approached the problem from programming
languages. The decentralized label model (Myers and Liskov 1997) and
Fabric (Liu et al. 2009) let mutually distrusting principals share
persistent objects under confidentiality and integrity labels. They
enforce these labels by placing cleartext only on trusted nodes, over
mutable, store-identified objects, and they do not quantify what
sustaining a policy costs. Our formalization follows this tradition: a
*link class* is a label on a lattice in the style of the decentralized
label model, which we extend from confidentiality and integrity
components to persistence and attribution components, so that
ordering, join, and the proof techniques of label lattices carry
over. We express persistence and integrity components as safety and
liveness obligations over a content-addressed store; confidentiality
components reuse the simulation-based definitions of T4.2.

**Link classes.** Table 1 fixes the link-class vocabulary that WP3
translates and T4.1's structural typing enforces.

| Class | Axis | Resolution guarantee | Minimal coordination; precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Classic Jumplink* | ladder | nothing: location-addressed, mutable | none; the Web default and the fallback outside any island |
| *Archived/Resilient Link* | ladder | stays resolvable across edits and removals; not verified | redundant snapshots and signatures (LOCKSS, Memento, Perma.cc, robust links) |
| *Trusted Link* | ladder | registrant known and accountable; bidirectionally tracked | island link registry (Microcosm/DLS lineage) |
| *Verified Link* | ladder | the exact cited span at the pinned version, or visible failure | content-addressed store (Git/IPFS, SWHID) |
| *Cryptographic Link* | ladder | a verified derivation from data the client never sees | secret-shared targets, SMPC/ZK resolution (PEP-LCA) |
| *Attributed Link* | attribution | reference bound to a verified author | identity binding (PROV, C2PA, eIDAS 2.0 wallets) |
| *Anonymous/Pseudonymous Link* | attribution | eligibility verified without identity | anonymous credentials, revocable pseudonyms |
| *Credentialed Link* | trust | an issuer's *verifiable credential* about the source, with its current revocation status | W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0, SD-JWT (RFC 9901), eIDAS 2.0 wallets; island-defined list of accepted issuers |
| *Endorsed/Reputation Link* | trust | decay-bounded, Sybil-resistant score returned with the target | retractable endorsement web (nanopublication IDEBT) |
| *Purpose-Bound Link* | usage | derivation valid for a stated recipient, purpose, and window; re-resolved on expiry | terms carried in the reference, in ODRL 2.2 with DPV purposes; assessed by compliance margin (transcopyright, insight economy) |
| *Capability/Affordance Link* | modality | an action, exposed only while valid; any rung applies | T4.3 agent gateway (*signifiers*) |
| *Static Link* | *link time* | interface bound at design time in a fixed binary encoding; no run-time discovery; fails visibly if either side changes | design-time agreement on schema and encoding; static linking (Levine 2000); WoT TD binary protocol bindings (e.g., Modbus), WoT Binding Templates |
| *Live/Subscription Link* | narrower | notification when the held version is superseded | WebSub-style hub per island |
| *Personalized/Disclosed Link* | narrower | resolution varies by requester, with the criteria disclosed | disclosed-filter design; counterpart to undisclosed platform curation |

*Table 1. The link-class family that WP2 formalizes, WP3 translates, and T4.1 enforces.*
Retraction is a state, not a class: content stays immutable in the
substrate, and a superseding retraction record changes the default
resolution. This is how a Verified or *Archived Link* honours GDPR Art.
17 without breaking the history on which other references depend.

**Proof obligations.** For each link class, the model must discharge
*realizability* and *necessity*. *Realizability:* given an island
that satisfies coordination requirement set C under threat model T, a
conforming implementation that resolves a reference of class L either
returns a result meeting L's guarantee or fails visibly, with no
silent downgrade. *Necessity:* dropping any element of C admits an
execution in which L's guarantee is violated without visible failure.
Necessity keeps the ladder honest, since it states that a rung cannot
be attained at lower coordination cost.

**Coordination cost and economic cost.** We measure coordination cost
in the standing obligations above and instrument it directly in the
T4.1 benchmarks. Economic cost enters with the *usage* axis: a
Purpose-Bound Link's terms cannot be enforced after resolution, so the
model scores such bundles by the margin by which compliance is less
costly than defection for the party able to defect. Operationally, we
compare the cost of re-requesting a fresh derivation with the cost of
storing, indexing, and re-using a stale one, per actor class and from
the WP1 stakeholder data. Transcopyright (Nelson 1997) set the terms of reuse in this
way, and current data-economy proposals set the terms of derived
insights in the same way; our contribution is to treat the margin as a
term in a *guarantee calculus*, so that the theory can return "this
guarantee is not attainable for this actor" as a first-class answer.

**Falsification protocol.** At each annual integration milestone, the
reconciliation document lists every design decision that the current
model could not derive. We triage each such decision as a model gap
(the calculus is extended), a model error (a claim is retracted, and
the retraction is published), or an implementation artifact (no model
change), and we report the count and disposition of these decisions,
so that the model's revision history is visible.

**Deliverables.** Formal model and proofs; the link-class taxonomy as
a citable specification; decision procedures in the form of a design
questionnaire that maps an island's size, stake, threat model, and
actor mix onto an attainable bundle; and the *island design handbook*
that consolidates them with the WP1 account and the WP3 translation
table.

### 2.3 WP3 — Border checks: translating NFPs at island borders (M6–M48)

*Method: protocol design with formal verification and conformance
testing; in parallel with WP2.*

**Translation table.** For every link class of Table 1 and every
NFP-supporting concept (versions and hashes, identities and
credentials, issuer lists, usage terms, encodings), WP3 determines
what happens at each border direction: inbound from the open Web,
outbound to it, and between two *islands*. Each entry is an upgrade, a
downgrade, or a refusal. Upgrades happen at admission: a *Classic
Jumplink* becomes a *Verified Link* once its target is snapshotted,
hashed, and registered, and a claim becomes a *Credentialed Link*
once an issuer that the island accepts has attested it. Downgrades
happen on exit: a Verified or *Cryptographic Link* degrades to a
Classic Jumplink for a non-member client, carrying a text fragment
(Burris and Bokan 2023) that keeps the cited passage addressable where
the page still contains it, and interoperates with Memento and Robust
Links; a Cryptographic Link releases only its derived value; and a
*Purpose-Bound Link* carries its terms, in ODRL with DPV purposes, so
that they remain readable outside the island. Island F's border
translates between binary protocol bindings and the uniform interface,
following the WoT Binding Templates (Koster and Korkan 2025), so that
its *Static Links* appear on the open Web as ordinary resources.
Between two islands, translation maps one bundle onto another, e.g.,
whether Island L accepts a credential issued in Island M, based on the
issuer lists and usage vocabularies that each island publishes as part
of its NFP set. The governing rule is that every downgrade is visible:
no reference may lose a guarantee silently.

**Admission protocol.** Inbound, an *admission protocol* (the *border
crossing*) lets a client discover the island's NFP set and bind to
it. The binding is a *credible commitment* in the sense of the W3C
TAG's *User Agents* note (Yasskin and Capadisli 2026): the client's
user agent restricts the use of the island's content, and nothing
beyond it, and keeps that restriction after admission. Here the island
applies its attribution regime. Clients present credentials in the
W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 data model, with selective disclosure
through SD-JWT (RFC 9901) or BBS-style signatures; browser clients
request the presentation through the W3C Digital Credentials API
(Caceres et al. 2026), so that the European Digital Identity Wallet
can serve as a client from the start. The island publishes the
issuers it accepts as part of its NFP set, and the registry checks
revocation status at resolution time. Agents cross the same border
through T4.3's gateway.

**Formal conditions.** In label terms, upgrades and downgrades are
endorsement and declassification, and the border must satisfy a
nonmalleability condition of the kind Cecchetti, Myers, and Arden
(2017) formalize: no outsider may influence what the island releases,
and the island may not endorse a claim whose content it cannot read.
The second half of this condition forces WP2's model to treat
admission under an anonymous credential explicitly, and it makes the
border crossing a proof obligation. We state the visibility rule above
as a property of the translation table and verify it for every entry.

**Evaluation.** A conformance test suite checks every table entry
against the reference implementation, including adversarial inputs
(replayed, revoked, or forged credentials; tampered snapshots; expired
terms). We measure the latency and storage cost that each upgrade adds
at admission, the share of outbound references that keep a resolvable
passage after downgrade, measured on the WP5 corpora over the project
period, and interoperability with the W3C specifications listed above.

**Deliverables.** Translation table v1 (M18) and v2 (M36); the
admission protocol specification; border gateways for Islands L, S, M,
and F; and the conformance test suite.

### 2.4 WP4 — Island technologies (M12–M54)

*Method: systems research, cryptographic protocol design with proofs,
and controlled comparative evaluation.* From the WP1 *islands* and the
WP2 *link classes*, we deduce the technologies the islands require.

**T4.1 Reference layer.** We pin span-level identifiers to immutable
versions in a Merkle-DAG store built on Git and IPFS primitives and on
Software Heritage's ISO-standardized SWHIDs. Clients reach island
resources through the Linked Web Storage protocol that the W3C LWS
Working Group standardizes from Solid (Wright and Bremer 2026), and
T4.1 contributes version-pinned identifiers to it as a profile. An
island-scoped bidirectional link registry makes explicit the
coordination that Xanadu wanted globally. Transclusion records
provenance in a W3C PROV-compatible form. Every reference is typed by
its WP2 link class, and typing is structural: resolving a Verified or
*Cryptographic Link* returns the exact cited span, or a verified
derivation of it, or fails visibly. Purpose-binding terms travel in
the reference itself, expressed in the W3C ODRL policy language
(Iannella and Villata 2018) with purposes from the Data Privacy
Vocabulary of the W3C DPV Community Group (Golpayegani, Flake, and
Ryan 2026), and the registry records issuance (to whom, for what,
until when) as immutable history even though the derivation itself
expires. We benchmark the implementation per link class on resolution
latency, storage overhead, and link-maintenance cost, at island scales
of 10² to 10⁵ members. Baselines are a plain HTTP fetch for *Classic
Jumplinks*, a Memento/Perma-style archival pipeline for *Archived
Links*, and, for the registry, the measured editorial cost of manual
citation archiving in Island L. Workloads come from the WP5 corpora.

**T4.2 Confidential references.** We generalize the PEP-LCA results
(Grau 2025) into a *resolution protocol family*: references whose
targets are secret-shared across island members and whose resolution
is a joint computation with a verifiable result. SMPC has been
deployed for single, fixed functions since the Danish sugar-beet
auction (Bogetoft et al. 2009) and in Estonian government statistics
(Bogdanov et al. 2016), and general-purpose protocols and
implementations such as SPDZ (Damgård et al. 2012) and MP-SPDZ
(Keller 2020) make arbitrary circuits practical. We build on MP-SPDZ
and contribute what these systems lack: a resolution protocol bound to
a citable, version-pinned reference. The nearest system precedent,
Fabric (§2.2), places cleartext only on nodes the data owner trusts;
our protocol family removes that requirement, since no node holds a
Cryptographic Link's target in full. Viaduct (Acay et al. 2021) shows
that the choice among multi-party computation, zero-knowledge proofs,
and commitments can be derived from labels automatically; we take the
same stance at the granularity of a reference, with the WP2 link class
selecting the protocol. Three arcs run partly in parallel. *(i) From
honest-but-curious to malicious security:* we use verifiable secret
sharing and zero-knowledge correctness proofs, and state and prove
security in standard simulation-based models against a static, then an
adaptive, adversary; the deliverable is a quantified
privacy-performance frontier. *(ii) Breaking availability coupling:*
in the PEP-LCA deployment, resolution requires enough members to be
online simultaneously, which obstructs interactive use; we investigate
threshold reconstruction and precomputed correlated randomness so that
resolution tolerates member downtime, and we treat the resulting
availability guarantee as an explicit WP2 coordination requirement.
*(iii) Input integrity:* committed inputs and attested sources that
connect to C2PA-style hardware roots make a verified derivation over
garbage inputs distinguishable from one over attested inputs. We
measure performance on topologies drawn from Island S's actual
supply-chain structure and report resolution latency against member
count, share-reconstruction threshold, and circuit depth. K.
Mitrokotsa and N. Cheng, who co-supervised the PEP-LCA work, continue
as external cryptographic collaborators.

**T4.3 Agents and static bindings.** The island's agent gateway exposes
capabilities as state-dependent *affordances*, through a HATEOAS-style
layer over MCP-class agent protocols, with semantics for *why* an
action is currently valid and explicit ordering guarantees for
dependent state-changing calls. Each affordance carries the WP2 link
class of the resource it exposes, so that an agent can distinguish a
Verified citation from a Classic Jumplink before acting. An agent
crosses the WP3 border by accepting the island's NFP set and
presenting a *verifiable credential* that states on whose behalf it
acts and within which limits, so that identity, delegated authority,
and obligations bind before any state-changing call. For Island F,
the same descriptions bind interfaces at design time as *Static Links*
over binary protocol bindings. *Evaluation design:* we compare, within
each scenario, (a) statically linked tool catalogues, (b) our
affordance layer, (c) hybrids, and (d) Static Links. Scenarios come
from Islands L, S, and M and from Island F's installations in building
automation (Siemens) and edge computing (AFarEdge). Dependent measures
are task success rate; latency per interaction; safety violations,
operationalized as state-changing actions on references whose declared
class does not support them, ordering violations on dependent calls,
and the attack success rate under the indirect-prompt-injection tasks
of AgentDojo (Debenedetti et al. 2024; Greshake et al. 2023), adapted
to island scenarios; and evolvability, measured by re-running a fixed
task suite after a scripted change to the island's capability surface
and recording the share of tasks that succeed without client
modification. Model choice, prompt, and random seed are held fixed
across conditions and reported. **Hypotheses:** affordance-scoped
exposure outperforms statically linked catalogues on safety and
evolvability without a significant loss in task success; Static Links
outperform both on latency in installations that do not change.
Together, the conditions measure the trade-off that the WP2 link-time
axis captures, and hence which *link time* the calculus should
recommend for a given installation. We treat a negative result as
publishable (see §5). Standardization proceeds through the W3C
Autonomous Agents on the Web Community Group, extending our work on
*signifiers*, and through the AI Agent Protocol Community Group for the
agent credential. Static Links go to the W3C Web of Things Working
Group as a profile of its protocol binding templates, through the Web
of Things channel that the PI's Chips JU role already funds (§3).

malicious-secure. Island S can begin measurement before T4.1 is
